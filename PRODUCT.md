# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Business owners, mostly of small and mid-sized service companies (roofing, insurance, senior care are the ones worked with so far), who are deciding whether to hire Melisa Mikko personally to build and run their websites, automations and internal systems. They are not technical. They arrive from a referral, LinkedIn, or a link she sends, usually to check that she is real and has done this before.

Confirmed by Melisa 2026-11-15: the site is for owners who hire her directly, not a funnel to Fieldhouse Ops and not a resume for employers.

## Product Purpose

melisamikko.com is Melisa's personal portfolio. It shows what she has built for real businesses as case studies, tells visitors who she is and how she works, and gets an interested owner to book a call with her. Success is a booked call. It will also hold her blog, which she plans to use to organize her thinking in public.

## Positioning

One person who both designs and ships the system and then keeps it running: production websites that generate leads, the automations and reporting behind them, internal tools that replace spreadsheets, and AI agents that do routine work under her approval. Her background is operations and people leadership, so the systems are built around how a team actually works.

## Operating Context

- Next.js 16 app on Vercel (project `folioooo`), repo `melisamikko/portfolio`, deploys from `main`.
- All copy lives in typed data files under `src/data/`.
- Fieldhouse Ops (fieldhouseops.com) is her agency and has its own site; this site is about her.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Clients are anonymized for now** (her decision 2026-11-15): no client names, logos, links or recognizable screenshots. Describe them by industry and place, for example "a San Antonio roofing company". Names may be added later with permission.
- No outcome numbers have been collected for any case study yet. A number appears only when it is measured and she has cleared it.
- Booking link: undecided. Fieldhouse Ops uses a Google Calendar booking page; her own link for this site has not been confirmed.
- Contact email: undecided (the live site still shows a placeholder).

## Evidence on Hand

Case-study material, all from her own project folders on MelSSD and verified there:

1. A roofing company's production website that generates nearly all of its leads (Next.js; 788 of her own commits on main as of 2026-11-15; speed work, city pages, a Meta-ads landing page with lead tracking into the CRM).
2. Not a case study. The insurance contracting and onboarding hub was checked on 2026-11-15: its commit history is almost entirely a colleague's (about 200 commits against 3 of hers), so it is left off the site unless she says which part was hers.
3. A roofing company's commercial sales data moved from a field-service system into a CRM pipeline with a live, access-protected sales dashboard.
4. A Slack-to-live website desk: a client posts a request in Slack, agents build and review the change, she approves by risk level, and a named desk bot tells the client it is live. Parts have not yet run on a real client request.
5. A senior care company's website forms moved from three Zapier automations to one self-hosted workflow, plus a daily KPI report in Slack and failure alerts.
6. A production tracker that replaced a daily routine of downloading reports from two systems into Excel. Whether it is hosted live is unconfirmed.
7. A life insurance site template (quote funnel, lead API) used to launch more than one agency site.

Older work from the previous site, usable as background: a company website rebuild off WordPress (100+ pages), an agent resource hub that replaced an intranet for about 100 agents, a performance-metrics reference doc, a 13-week call center launch plan, and a tech stack risk audit.

Absent and not to be fabricated: testimonials, revenue or lead figures, client logos, headshots, certifications (the old site's were placeholders).

## Product Principles

1. Show the work doing its job; every claim traces to something she built.
2. Speak to an owner, not an engineer: what changed for the business comes before how it was built.
3. Honest scope: say what is hers, what is still early, and what has no number yet.
4. The person is part of the offer: her voice and how she works with teams belong on the site.
5. One action: book a call.
